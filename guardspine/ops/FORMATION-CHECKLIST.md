## DANGER ZONES

### 1. 83(b) Election -- 30-Day Hard Deadline (HIGHEST PRIORITY)

**What it is:** When you purchase restricted stock subject to vesting, the IRS lets you elect to pay tax on the stock's value at grant date (pennies) instead of at vesting date (potentially millions). This is called an 83(b) election.

**Why it matters for GuardSpine:** You are buying 4,000,000 shares at $0.0001/share = $400 total. At grant, the company is worth approximately $0. If you do NOT file 83(b) and the company is worth $10M when shares vest, you owe ordinary income tax on the difference -- potentially $100K+ per founder in phantom income you cannot sell to cover.

**The deadline:** 30 calendar days from the date the stock is granted (board approval date, not the date you sign paperwork). Weekends and holidays count. NO extensions. NO exceptions. NO late filings accepted.

**How to file (2026):**

1. Create IRS online account at irs.gov (requires ID.me verification with photo ID + selfie)
2. Go to irs.gov/dmaf/form/f15620
3. Fill out IRS Form 15620 electronically
4. Submit online and save confirmation
5. Give a copy to the company (email is fine)
6. Attach a copy to your personal tax return for that year

**Backup method:** Print Form 15620, fill it out, and mail via certified mail (return receipt requested) to the IRS Service Center where you file your personal return. Certified mail postmark = proof of timely filing.

**Both David AND Igor must file separately.**

**If Kristen receives shares for less than fair market value, she should also file 83(b) -- but since she is receiving fully vested shares and FMV is ~$0 at formation, this is less critical. Consult tax advisor.**

### 2. IP Assignment -- Must Happen Before First Investor Conversation

**What happens if you skip it:** Every line of GuardSpine code currently belongs to David and Igor personally, not to any company. If an investor asks "does the company own its IP?" the answer is currently "no." This kills deals.

**What to assign:**

- guardspine-kernel (TypeScript, canonical)
- guardspine-kernel-py (Python port)
- codeguard-action (GitHub Action, 737 tests)
- guardspine-spec (v0.2.1 schema)
- All proprietary UI, integrations, and tooling
- All trade secrets, algorithms, and processes
- Domain names (guardspine.com, etc.)
- Any related inventions or provisional patent interests

**Igor's situation:** Igor is a technical co-builder. His IP assignment is especially critical because he wrote core kernel code. The assignment must be comprehensive and cover all prior work.

**Timeline:** Execute IP assignment the same day you issue founder stock. There is no legal reason to delay, and every day without it is a day your company does not own its core technology.

### 3. Logan MOU Re-Execution

**Risk:** The MOU with Proprioceptive AI (55 provisional patents) was signed by David personally, not by a corporate entity. Once GuardSpine, Inc. exists, this agreement should be re-executed entity-to-entity so the IP relationship is with the company, not with David as an individual.

**Timeline:** Not as urgent as 83(b) or IP assignment, but should be done within 30 days of incorporation. Contact Logan, explain the corporate formation, and provide a restated MOU for countersignature.

### 4. Corporate Veil -- Keep It Clean From Day 1

**Never commingle personal and corporate funds.** Once the Mercury account is open, ALL company expenses go through it. No more paying for servers, domains, or SaaS tools from personal accounts without reimbursement documentation.

**Hold board meetings** (even if informal) and document decisions. Annual board consent at minimum. Clerky provides templates.

### 5. Delaware Franchise Tax -- March 1 Annual Deadline

**If you incorporate in 2026, your first franchise tax is due March 1, 2027.** The minimum is $225 ($50 filing + $175 tax). Late = $200 penalty + 1.5%/month interest. Set a calendar reminder for February 1, 2027.

**Use the Assumed Par Value Capital method** when calculating (not the default Authorized Shares method). For a startup with minimal assets and 10M authorized shares, the Assumed Par Value method almost always results in the minimum $400 or less, while the Authorized Shares method can produce bills of $8,000+. Clerky and most formation services will remind you of this.
